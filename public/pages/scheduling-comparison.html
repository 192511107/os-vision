OS Heuristic Assessment: Context-Dependent Performance

The highlighted metrics above represent the measured empirical performance strictly for the currently configured process workload. In Operating Systems principles, no single CPU scheduling algorithm is universally superior across all computing scenarios:

◆ Shortest Job First (SJF) & SRTF: Provably minimize average waiting time when burst times are known in advance. However, long-burst processes can suffer severe starvation under continuous short-job arrival streams.
◆ Round Robin (RR): Guarantees fair, starvation-free CPU time sharing and low response latency for interactive desktop and cloud environments, at the expense of higher turnaround time due to frequent context switches.
◆ First-Come, First-Served (FCFS): Simple and starvation-free with zero preemption overhead. However, it is vulnerable to the Convoy Effect, where short jobs wait for a long CPU-bound job.
◆ Priority Scheduling: Enforces business and real-time urgency requirements. Low-priority jobs can starve indefinitely unless dynamic aging heuristics are deployed.
